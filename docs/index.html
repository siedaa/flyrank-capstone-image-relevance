AI Image Understanding & Content Matching Engine

A FastAPI service that uses Google Gemini's vision model to match blog posts to animal images by meaning — and refuses to guess when it isn't confident.

The Problem

Blog posts need images, but finding the right one is tedious. Existing tools match by filename or tags — they can't understand that a post about "The Secret Life of Red Foxes" should be paired with a fox photo, not a wolf or a bear.

This system takes a different approach: it uses AI vision to understand what each image actually shows, then matches images to posts by semantic meaning. Crucially, it knows when to say "I don't know" — rejecting suggestions where no image confidently fits.

The Safety Mechanism in Action

The mismatch guard is the core safety feature. Even when a wolf image scores high similarity against a fox post, the category check catches the mismatch and rejects it:

Forcing wolf_02.jpg as a candidate for 'The Secret Life of Red Foxes'
Similarity score: 0.7683
Guard verdict: rejected
Reason: Category mismatch: expected fox, detected gray wolf

This test was deliberately constructed to try to fool the system. The guard holds.

Honest caveats: The eval set is intentionally small (7 posts), matching the brief's minimal scope — a single misclassification would drop the score to 85.7%. The similarity threshold (similarity_floor=0.75) was tuned by observing this same dataset rather than validated against a separate held-out set. Both are disclosed as honest scope limitations of a capstone-sized project, not hidden.
